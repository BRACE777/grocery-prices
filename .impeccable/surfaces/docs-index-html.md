## Scope

One surface: the weekly price page, read on a phone. Visitor mode: Operate.

## Audience and task

One shopper, Wednesday morning, at home, deciding which of two chains to shop
at and what to stock up on. They already know the usual pack price of every
line on the list, so the pack price is their anchor and the unit price is the
tiebreaker. Specials reset Wednesday.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The aisle's own shelf-edge ticket, carrying the one thing a real shelf
ticket cannot tell you: whether this price is actually unusual. It refuses the
comparison-dashboard card grid, and it refuses promoting price-per-100g to the
headline, because the shopper recognises pack prices by heart and a number they
do not recognise cannot anchor a decision.

OWN-WORLD: A white die-cut ticket card seated in a grey plastic rail, one
ticket per chain, green rail for Woolworths and red for Coles. Heavy condensed
grotesque throughout. The pack price is set at ticket scale; the legally
mandated unit-price line sits small beneath it. Specials wear the chains' own
yellow flash, half price its own heavier treatment, multibuys their own. The
page's single non-retailer mark is a rarity stamp, struck on the ticket like an
inspector's mark, which is the app speaking rather than the shop.

STORY: The shopper sees what dropped this week, learns which of those drops are
genuinely rare rather than the usual fortnightly cycle, and leaves knowing
which shop to visit and which two or three things to buy deep.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A masthead naming the week and the run time. Then the stock-up
board: up to three flash tickets ranked by how unusual the discount is, the
first full width and the other two paired. Then the first bay sign and the
first pair of tickets seated in their rail.

FORM: Shelf ticket, candidate 1 of my grounded list, taken by the user over the
assigned roll. Seed key 23a2dcaa.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the
finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its
provenance

## Motion

One system, the aisle being set before opening. Focal moment: the stock-up
board's lead ticket is stamped down. Supporting: tickets slot into their rail
from the left, the cheaper of each pair then rises proud so the comparison
resolves visibly, special flashes turn over onto the price, bay signs
illuminate as they are reached, and a pressed ticket seats into the rail.
Nothing is hidden in the default state; every effect is layered onto a page
that is already finished.
